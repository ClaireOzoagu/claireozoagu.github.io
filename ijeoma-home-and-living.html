01 — The caseGreat furniture, a broken journey

Ijeoma Home & Living is a furniture and decor retailer with showrooms in Lekki, Surulere and Abuja, plus a growing online store. Founder Mrs. Ijeoma Chukwu grew it from a small sofa workshop into a mid-market brand known for made-to-order pieces. Operations Manager Mr. Ibrahim Yakubu was asked to review the whole customer experience, from the first welcome to after-delivery support.

First-time custom orders go through a "Welcome Process": a deposit, a measurement visit and a written order confirmation. The written confirmation was the weakest step.

39%of 248 orders got a correct written confirmation
44%of 97 customers asked could state their delivery date
31online reviews about unclear delivery timing
58Instagram messages about price differences
19incidents where installers missed agreed instructions

Customer interviews showed the journey breaking at every stage: a two-day wait for an Instagram reply followed by a different price in the showroom, no written confirmation after a 60% deposit, different completion dates on every call, a late delivery, installers with no record of the instructions, and no follow-up afterwards.

Yet every customer said the furniture itself met or exceeded expectations. The dissatisfaction was entirely about how they were treated along the way.

02 — The bigger problemA good product isn't enough

A business can make an excellent product and still lose customers. Promises made verbally weren't confirmed in writing, updates were inconsistent and nobody followed up after delivery.

The question is not "Is the furniture good?" but "Does the customer feel informed and looked after from their first message to after delivery?"

It was also a visibility problem. Feedback lived in staff members' memories and scattered online comments, so management couldn't see the pattern or act on it.

03 — My taskWhat I set out to do

  • Use the Customer Onboarding Framework to find which steps of the Welcome Process were failing, diagnose the root cause, and redesign it so customers get written clarity early.
  • Classify the feedback channels, explain the risk of the current uncoordinated approach, and design a simple, low-cost Voice of the Customer process with the top two issues to fix first.
  • Map the customer journey, identify the "moments of truth" that decide whether a customer returns or recommends the brand, and propose a low-cost fix for each pain point.
  • Show how the onboarding gaps, feedback and journey pain points connect, and present my top three recommendations to the founder in priority order.

04 — What I builtOne tracked customer journey

I designed the journey so every customer is tracked from first enquiry through production, delivery and after-sales care, and nothing depends on someone remembering.

01

One front door

Website form, Instagram DMs and the showroom kiosk all feed one 6-stage pipeline in the CRM, with quality gates so an order can't move forward until the right details are captured.

02

Written confirmation, linked to payment

60/40 deposit and balance invoicing, with a simple rule: no recorded deposit, no timber cut. The customer gets fabric, dimensions and delivery date in writing.

03

Proactive production updates

Milestone emails across the 5-week build, plus a QA extension notice if quality checks need more time, so customers don't have to call for updates.

04

Delivery that matches the promise

A dispatch docket goes out 2 days before delivery, carrying every special instruction agreed in the showroom to the installers.

05

After-sales care and Voice of the Customer

A satisfaction survey goes out 24 hours after installation. A rating under 4 stars opens a priority ticket with a 24-hour callback. Health scoring and 4 dashboards give management the visibility they were missing.
